Practical Concept:
Scenario: hr-ws41.cbx.lab (10.94.40.63), an HR workstation, was compromised and connected out to 198.51.100.77 at 01:58. Using the non-admin domain account l.haddad, the attacker bound to the spoolss interface on prt01 over \pipe\spoolss at 02:16:55 and called RpcAddPrinterDriverEx at 02:17:03 with a pDriverPath pointing at a file staged on hr-ws41's share. prt01 (missing the July 2021 update and with Point and Print NoWarningNoElevationOnInstall = 1) copied prnmgrx64.dll into C:\Windows\System32\spool\drivers\x64\3\ (Sysmon 11 at 02:17:05, Image spoolsv.exe), logged PrintService Operational 316 and loaded the unsigned DLL into spoolsv.exe (Sysmon 7, Signed false, at 02:17:06). spoolsv.exe (running as NT AUTHORITY\SYSTEM) started rundll32.exe at 02:17:09 (Sysmon 1 / 4688), which ran net.exe and added the new local account prnsupport to Administrators at 02:17:41 (4732, Subject PRT01$). The same call pattern from 10.94.40.63 then hit prt02 (02:24) and lab-fs03 (02:29), both of which stored a file with the identical SHA-256. prt03 is patched with RestrictDriverInstallationToAdministrators = 1 and refused the call (PrintService Admin 808 with error 0x5 access denied, no file written).
